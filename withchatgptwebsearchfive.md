**4.** **What I'd measure from the 30+ responses**

Your most important output shouldn't be "30 people said X."

Build a simple **hypothesis validation matrix:**

| Hypothesis                   | Survey question |                         Validation threshold |
| ---------------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------: |
| Awareness is a problem       | Q5              |                >25% didn't know Voice exists |
| Accuracy is a problem        | Q6              |                >30% select accuracy/language |
| Privacy is a problem         | Q6 + Q9         |               >30% select privacy/discomfort |
| Typing gives more control    | Q6              |                  >30% select editing/control |
| Voice lacks compelling value | Q6              |                      >30% select "no reason" |
| Environment matters          | Q8 + Q9         |             Strong difference by environment |
| Regional language matters    | Q10 + Q6        | Higher barrier among regional-language users |

**The most important comparison**

Don't just calculate overall percentages.

Compare:

**Regular Voice users vs non-regular Voice users**

For example:

Does privacy distinguish Voice users from non-users?

Does perceived accuracy distinguish them?

Does "typing is easier" distinguish them?

Does language preference distinguish them?

That gets you much closer to **causality than a simple frequency table**.

**5. What existing research suggests you may find**

There is already a useful directional signal: one recent 31-student India study reported **97% awareness, 81% having tried Voice, but only 6% using it regularly**. Another 55+ respondent study reported **94.7% awareness, 78.9% trial, but only 12.3% regular usage**. These are independent, non-representative studies—not evidence you should copy into your results—but they strongly suggest testing **retention/experience rather than assuming awareness is the main problem**.

So your research question becomes:

**"Why do students who know about and sometimes try Voice fail to make it their default interaction mode?"**

That's a much stronger PM research question.
