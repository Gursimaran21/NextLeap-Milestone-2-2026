## Milestone 2 - ChatGPT : Why Users are Not using Voice Input on ChatGPT Mobile, especially in India

### Solution to ChatGPT Problem Framing, Ideation & Validation + Metrics: Why Users are Not using Voice Input on ChatGPT Mobile, especially in India

#### Task 2 - Lay out 4–5 user segments who are likely to use ChatGPT mobile (e.g., students, job seekers, content creators, regional language users)

For a **ChatGPT Mobile India** product case, I’d segment users by **need/job-to-be-done**, not just demographics. The data strongly supports a young, learning- and work-oriented user base: 18–34-year-olds account for about 80% of consumer ChatGPT messages in India, while work-related usage is ~35%.

**5 high-potential ChatGPT Mobile user segments**

| Segment                                            | Who they are                                                  | Core ChatGPT jobs                                                             | Why Mobile matters                                   | Voice potential |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| 🎓 **1. Students & Learners**                      | College students, exam aspirants, young learners              | Learn concepts, exam prep, summarize notes, solve problems, tutoring          | Study anywhere; camera + voice + chat are useful     | **Very High**   |
| 💼 **2. Job Seekers & Early-Career Professionals** | Graduates, job seekers, 18–34 professionals                   | Resume, interview prep, job applications, workplace communication, upskilling | Frequent mobile job/work activity                    | **High**        |
| 💻 **3. Tech & Knowledge Workers**                 | Developers, analysts, engineers, IT professionals             | Coding, debugging, data analysis, research, work productivity                 | Mobile for quick questions; desktop for complex work | **Medium–High** |
| 🎨 **4. Content Creators & Creatives**             | YouTubers, Instagram creators, writers, marketers             | Brainstorming, scripts, captions, ideas, editing, content planning            | Mobile is their primary creation/distribution device | **High**        |
| 🗣️ **5. Regional-Language & Voice-First Users**   | Users who prefer Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, etc. | Translation, learning, everyday questions, writing, accessibility             | Mobile is often the primary internet device          | **Very High**   |

**1.** 🎓 **Students & Learners — Highest-priority segment**

This should probably be your **#1 segment.**

OpenAI says India has the **largest student population on ChatGPT globally**, with millions using it for homework help, exam preparation and exploring ideas. OpenAI has also launched an India-focused Learning Accelerator and Study Mode based partly on feedback from Indian learners.

**Typical prompts:**

- "Explain this like I'm a beginner."
- "Quiz me on this chapter."
- "Solve this step by step."
- "Help me prepare for tomorrow's exam."
- "Explain this in Hindi/Tamil."

**Voice opportunity:**
Extremely strong for **interactive tutoring, language learning and asking follow-up questions.**

**Key pain point:**
Accuracy + ability to interrupt/rephrase + regional language support.

**2.** 💼 **Job Seekers & Early-Career Professionals**

India's ChatGPT audience is heavily concentrated among younger users: 18–24-year-olds account for just under half of messages, while 18–34 accounts for roughly 80%.

This makes **career acceleration** an important mobile use case.

**Typical prompts:**

- "Improve my resume."
- "Prepare me for this interview."
- "Explain this job description."
- "Write a LinkedIn message."
- "Teach me Excel."
- "Help me switch careers."

**Voice opportunity: High**

Voice is particularly useful for:

- mock interviews
- English practice
- interview role-play
- explaining career problems
- brainstorming answers

**Key pain point:**

Users want to **think aloud**, but still need the ability to edit/refine the final output.

**3.** 💻 **Tech & Knowledge Workers**

This segment includes India's huge IT/technology workforce.

India is particularly strong in technical ChatGPT usage: OpenAI reports that Indian users ask coding-related questions at roughly **3× the global median**, while data-analysis usage among Plus/Pro users is around **4× the global average.**

**Typical users:**

- Software developers
- Data analysts
- Engineers
- Product managers
- Researchers
- IT professionals

**Typical prompts:**

"Debug this code."

"Explain this API."

"Analyze this CSV."

"Summarize this technical document."

**Voice opportunity: Medium–High**

Voice is excellent for:

"Explain why this code is failing."

But typing/pasting remains superior for **code-heavy tasks.**

So I'd position Voice as a **thinking/diagnostic layer**, not a replacement for text.

**4.** 🎨 **Content Creators & Creatives**

This includes:

- YouTubers
- Instagram creators
- Bloggers
- Writers
- Social-media managers
- Freelancers
- Marketing professionals

ChatGPT's global usage research shows that writing is among its most common activities, while creative/self-expression use is also a meaningful category.

**Typical prompts:**

"Give me 10 YouTube ideas."

"Turn this idea into a script."

"Make this Instagram caption better."

"Give me a hook for this video."

**Voice opportunity: High**

This is one of the best Voice use cases because creators often **think faster than they type.**

A compelling product experience could be:

**Speak → brainstorm → structure → generate**

rather than:

**Type → generate**

**5.** 🗣️ **Regional-Language & Voice-First Users**

This is the most interesting segment **for your specific Voice-adoption case.**

India's internet audience is extremely multilingual, and OpenAI has specifically highlighted improvements in Indic-language capabilities.

Think beyond "Hindi users."

A better definition is:

**Users who naturally think, speak or search in an Indian language or mixed-language format.**

Examples:

- Tamil + English
- Hindi + English
- Telugu + English
- Bengali + English
- Marathi + English
- Punjabi + English

**Typical prompts:**

"இந்த concept-ஐ simple-ஆ explain பண்ணு."

"Mujhe interview ke liye prepare karo."

"ఈ topic ని simple గా explain చేయి."

This segment has potentially the **highest incremental value from Voice** because speaking can reduce the friction of typing in a non-primary keyboard language.

**But there's a catch:**

Voice must accurately understand **Indian accents + code-switching + regional terminology.** Otherwise, the very feature intended to reduce friction creates more friction.

**The key PM insight**

Don't ask:

**"Which demographic should we target?"**

Ask:

**"Which users have a task where speaking is fundamentally better than typing?"**

That points to **three initial Voice beachheads:**

**1. Students → conversational tutoring**

**2. Regional-language users → low-friction multilingual interaction**

**3. Job seekers → interview + English practice**

Those three combine **large potential user bases + frequent mobile usage + a genuine reason to speak rather than type.**

This also aligns well with your earlier Voice-adoption hypothesis: the opportunity isn't simply to make more people try Voice; it's to create **repeatable moments where Voice is clearly superior to typing.**
