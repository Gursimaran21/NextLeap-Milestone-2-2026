## Milestone 2 - ChatGPT : Why Users are Not using Voice Input on ChatGPT Mobile, especially in India

### Solution to ChatGPT Problem Framing, Ideation & Validation + Metrics: Why Users are Not using Voice Input on ChatGPT Mobile, especially in India

#### Task 8 - Chosen segment and justification of impact

### Chosen Segment: Indian Students aged 18–24

**Segment definition**

**Primary target:** Mobile-first Indian students aged **18–24** who use ChatGPT for learning, homework, exam preparation, concept clarification, and career preparation—but primarily **type rather than use Voice**.

I would choose this segment over job seekers, content creators, or regional-language users because it combines **large scale + high frequency + strong Voice use cases + strategic importance to ChatGPT in India**.

### Why this segment has the highest potential impact

**1. Large existing ChatGPT usage base**

OpenAI's latest India analysis shows that users aged **18–24 generate just under half of all ChatGPT messages in India**. Combined, 18–34-year-olds generate about **80% of consumer ChatGPT messages** in India.

This means improving Voice for 18–24-year-olds can potentially affect a very large existing user base—not just acquire a new audience.

**2. India is ChatGPT's largest student market**

OpenAI says India has the **largest student population on ChatGPT globally**, with millions of students using it for homework, exam preparation, and exploring ideas.

That creates a strong existing **product–user fit** around learning.

**3. Learning creates repeat Voice opportunities**

Students have recurring tasks:

- Explain a concept
- Ask follow-up questions
- Practice English
- Prepare for interviews
- Solve doubts
- Take quizzes
- Brainstorm ideas

Unlike a one-off content-creation use case, these can happen **daily or multiple times per week**.

OpenAI's own research shows that **Practical Guidance**, which includes tutoring/teaching and how-to help, is one of the three dominant categories of ChatGPT usage globally.

**4. Voice has a genuine advantage for learning**

Voice can turn ChatGPT from:

**Question → Answer**

into:

**Question → Explanation → Follow-up → Practice → Feedback**

This is particularly valuable for language practice and conversational learning. Recent research with English-language learners found ChatGPT Voice offered a convenient, low-anxiety and flexible environment for speaking practice, although technical and feedback limitations remained.

**5. The problem appears to be adoption, not lack of relevance**

Recent India-focused user research on ChatGPT Voice has found that students may already know about or try Voice but still fail to become regular users. One independent study reported **69% had tried Voice**, while barriers included shared-space discomfort and other usability issues. Another independent project with 34 survey responses and six interviews identified trust, accent recognition, privacy and discoverability as barriers.

These aren't representative OpenAI datasets, so I would use them as **directional evidence**, not population estimates.

**Why students win**

**Reach × Frequency × Voice fit** is the key combination.

A student can encounter a Voice opportunity **every day:**

"Explain this."

"Quiz me."

"I'm confused about this."

"Give me an example."

"Now explain it in Hindi."

"Ask me five questions."

That creates the potential for a **habit loop**, rather than simply increasing feature discovery.
