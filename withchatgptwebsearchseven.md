## Milestone 2 - ChatGPT : Why Users are Not using Voice Input on ChatGPT Mobile, especially in India

### Solution to ChatGPT Problem Framing, Ideation & Validation + Metrics: Why Users are Not using Voice Input on ChatGPT Mobile, especially in India

#### Task 7 - Based on the insights, fill in the Problem Framing Canvas to clearly define the real barrier to voice usage

**Problem Framing Canvas — ChatGPT Voice Adoption in India**

Based on the research we've done, I would **reframe the problem away from “students don't know Voice exists.**” OpenAI identifies India as its largest student market for ChatGPT, with millions using it for homework, exam prep and exploration. Recent India-focused research also suggests that awareness can be relatively high while **regular Voice usage remains low**, pointing toward trust, context and workflow friction.

**1. 👤 Target User**

**Mobile-first Indian students aged 18–24**

- College/university students
- Exam and competitive-test aspirants
- Early-career learners
- Frequent ChatGPT users
- Often multilingual: English + Hindi/Hinglish/regional language
- Frequently use ChatGPT in hostels, homes, libraries, campuses and while commuting

This is a high-value segment because India has the world's largest student population on ChatGPT, and OpenAI is actively investing in India-focused learning experiences.

**2. 🎯 User Goal**

“**I want to get help from ChatGPT quickly and naturally while studying, without having to formulate and type every question.**”

The ideal experience is conversational:

**Ask → clarify → follow up → understand → practice**

OpenAI itself positions Voice as useful for academic work where talking through an idea is easier than typing, particularly for exploratory thinking and iterative discussion.

**3. 😣 Current Situation / Pain**

Students often **know about or have tried Voice**, but return to typing.

Why?

**Trust**

- Concern that Voice will misunderstand their words
- Accent/Hinglish/regional-language uncertainty
- Technical/academic terminology may be transcribed incorrectly

**Control**

- Typing allows them to formulate and edit before submitting
- Voice can feel like “say it correctly the first time”

**Privacy**

- Students frequently study in hostels, PGs, libraries and shared homes
- They don't want others hearing their questions

**Social comfort**

- Talking to an AI aloud can feel awkward in public/shared spaces

**Workflow mismatch**

- Studying often involves PDFs, screenshots, formulas, code and copied questions
- Typing + attachments can feel more natural than Voice alone

Research on multilingual Indian learners provides particularly strong evidence for the language problem: ASR errors have been observed with Indian-accented English, Hindi loanwords and Hindi/Hinglish code-switching, disrupting conversational flow.

**4. 🧠 User Mental Model**

This is the **most important part of the canvas**.

Non-regular users tend to think:

“**ChatGPT is a text/typing tool. Voice is basically another way to enter the prompt.**”

Therefore:

**Typing =**

- precise
- controllable
- private
- serious
- easy to edit

**Voice =**

- fast
- conversational
- potentially inaccurate
- socially visible
- harder to control

Regular Voice users have a different mental model:

“**Voice lets me think out loud with ChatGPT.**”

That distinction matters because OpenAI's current Voice experience is explicitly designed for natural back-and-forth conversation, brainstorming and exploration—not merely dictation.

**5. 🔍 Root Cause**

**Voice has not yet earned enough trust to overcome the convenience and control of typing.**

More specifically:

**Students perceive the expected benefit of Voice as “faster input,” but perceive the risks as “misunderstanding + lack of control + being overheard.”**

So the mental calculation becomes:

**Benefit:**
“Maybe I can say my question faster.”

**Risk:**
“What if it misunderstands me?”

**Risk:**
“What if someone hears me?”

**Risk:**
“What if I need to correct the question?”

Therefore:

**“I'll just type it.”**

**6. 💡 Key Insight**

The problem is **not simply Voice adoption**.

It's a **trust gap**.

**Current experience**

**Think → Type → Edit → Send → Trust**

**Desired Voice experience**

**Think → Speak naturally → ChatGPT understands → Converse → Learn**

The bridge between those two experiences is currently weak for some Indian student contexts.

Public India-focused research independently reports the same pattern: students cite privacy in shared spaces, Hinglish recognition and the greater perceived control of typing as barriers.

**7. 🚫 What is NOT the core problem?**

This is important for your PM case.

❌ “**Students don't know Voice exists.**”

Probably **not the primary root cause.**

Some India-focused research reports that awareness is already high—for example, one 31-student study reported 97% awareness but only 6% regular usage. Another 30-survey/5-interview study reported 83% awareness but only 9% regular use among its Hindi-medium student sample.

❌ “**Students don't want Voice.**”

Also too simplistic.

Students already use voice interactions elsewhere, and Voice has obvious use cases for brainstorming, language practice and interactive learning.

❌ “**Voice is inherently inferior.**”

No.

OpenAI specifically recommends Voice for academic situations where spoken iteration can be more natural than typing.

**8. 🏆 Final Problem Statement**

I'd put **this exact statement** on your Problem Framing Canvas:

**Mobile-first Indian students aged 18–24 need a faster, more natural way to interact with ChatGPT while learning, but they default to typing because Voice does not yet feel sufficiently linguistically reliable, socially private, or controllable in their everyday study environments.**

**Shorter PM version**

**Indian students don't reject Voice—they don't trust it enough to replace typing.**

And the three root barriers are:

**1. Linguistic trust**
"Will it understand how I actually speak?"

**2. Social/privacy trust**
"Can I use this without everyone hearing me?"

**3. Interaction control**
"Can I say what I mean and correct it easily?"

**9. Opportunity statement**

This leads naturally to the next stage:

**How might we make ChatGPT Voice feel as linguistically trustworthy, socially comfortable, and controllable as typing—while preserving the speed and conversational advantage of Voice?**

That is a much stronger opportunity than:

❌ “How might we increase Voice adoption?”

because it tells the product team **what must change in the user experience** to move the metric.
